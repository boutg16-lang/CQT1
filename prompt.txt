### STRICT VIRAL RULES (The "ViralCutter Standard"):
1.  **NO "GARBAGE" STARTS:**
    -   NEVER start with filler words ("Um", "Uh", "So...", "And then").
    -   NEVER start with low-energy intros ("Hi guys, welcome back").
    -   **ALWAYS** start with a Hook: A strong statement, a question, or an action verb.
    -   *Bad Start:* "(10s) So, I was thinking..."
    -   *Good Start:* "(12s) I almost died yesterday."

2.  **THE "STANDALONE" TEST:**
    -   Can this segment be shown to a stranger without ANY context?
    -   If it contains "That's why *he* said that" (and "he" is unknown), it FAILS. Eliminate or expand context.

3.  **NARRATIVE ARC:**
    -   **Start:** Hook (0-3s).
    -   **Middle:** Value/Story (Retain attention).
    -   **End:** Punchline/Conclusion (Satisfying ending). NEVER cut mid-sentence.

4.  **DURATION MATH:**
    -   Use the `(XXs)` tags to estimate duration.
    -   CONSTRAINT: Segment MUST be between {min_duration}s and {max_duration}s.
    -   Prefer a complete thought with a natural beginning and ending; never stretch a weak idea only to reach the minimum.

5.  **DIVERSITY AND NON-REPETITION:**
    -   Select distinct moments, not several versions of the same answer or story beat.
    -   Spread candidates across different topics, emotions, speakers, and hook types when the transcript allows it.
    -   Set `topic` to a short neutral topic label and `angle` to the editorial angle: lesson, surprise, story, opinion, mistake, result, question, or warning.
    -   Set `hook_type` to one of: question, bold_claim, story, problem, result, contrast, or quote.
    -   Set `novelty_score` from 0 to 100; lower it when the segment repeats another candidate.

6.  **PROFESSIONAL TITLES — NO CHEAP CLICKBAIT:**
    -   Write a clear title that creates curiosity without inventing facts, promising impossible results, or using misleading ALL CAPS.
    -   For Arabic, use natural modern Arabic, concise wording, and preserve the speaker's actual meaning. Avoid excessive emojis and punctuation.
    -   Keep the main title suitable for YouTube Shorts, and make each alternative title use a genuinely different angle rather than superficial synonym changes.
    -   Do not reveal a conclusion inaccurately. A title may create an information gap, but it must be supported by the clip.

7.  **CONTENT SAFETY (YouTube Policy — ABSOLUTE RULE, OVERRIDES VIRALITY):
    -   NEVER select a segment containing **hate speech**: slurs, insults or dehumanizing language targeting any race, religion, nationality, ethnicity, gender, or group (e.g. calling people "animals", "apes", "pigs", "filthy", "infidels" as an attack, etc.).
    -   NEVER select a segment containing **calls to violence or harm** ("kill them", "slaughter them", "they must be eliminated", "death to ...").
    -   NEVER select a segment with heavy profanity, sexual slurs, or harassment of an individual.
    -   Even if such a moment looks "viral", it will get the channel a YouTube strike — it is WORTHLESS. Skip it and find a clean alternative.
    -   Quoting hate speech to condemn it is only acceptable if the segment clearly frames it as condemnation; when in doubt, SKIP.

8.  **FACTUAL TITLES ONLY (CLIP TRANSCRIPT == THE ONLY SOURCE OF TRUTH):**
    -   The title must describe THE SELECTED CLIP, not the entire source video. Judge every title against the exact words spoken between the clip's own start and end — never against the rest of this transcript.
    -   Do not mention any fact, person, number, result, or claim that is not explicitly present in the clip's own transcript.
    -   Do not invent conclusions, outcomes, prices, statistics, names, or "before/after" results. If the clip does not state it, the title must not claim it.
    -   Do not choose a clip only because it contains a popular keyword; the clip must stand on its own as a complete moment.
    -   NEVER use empty clickbait such as "لن تصدق ما حدث", "هذه المعلومة ستغير حياتك", "سر خطير", "الجميع مخطئون", "اكتشاف صادم" (or the English equivalents) unless the clip's own words literally support that claim.
    -   If the context is insufficient or ambiguous, prefer a conservative descriptive title and LOWER the editorial scores — reject the clip instead of guessing.

9.  **BOUNDARIES (BEGIN AND END CLEANLY):**
    -   The clip must begin at a natural word or sentence boundary and end only after the idea is complete.
    -   Never start with an isolated conjunction (لكن، لذلك، لأن، وهذا، فهو، ثم، فإذا، ولكن / "so", "and then", "but") unless the sentence is fully understandable alone — prefer starting one sentence earlier.
    -   Never end on a preposition, a conjunction, or an unfinished clause — extend to the end of the sentence instead.
    -   Do not guess timestamps when the (XXs) tags already give you a reliable anchor.
